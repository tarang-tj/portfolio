Segment A · Corporate analytics

The Coca-Cola Company

AI MarTech Intern, Ignite Program · Global Human Insights · Atlanta · May 18 to Aug 14, 2026

A recurring analytical request took a four-person team about three days to pull. I interviewed 10 of the people who relied on it and built an internal tool to replace the manual pull.

Azure OpenAI plans the query. Deterministic Python computes every figure over a 25 to 30 million row backend. Model output is schema validated, and more than 50 reconciliation checks run across Excel, Power BI and Python. If two systems disagree, the run halts. The checks caught nine mismatches.

Along the way I traced a recurring discrepancy to the order in which figures were rounded, and wrote the calculation standard the team adopted. The tool went into daily production for the trademark brand quarterly reviews, and its output reaches more than 500 employees.

Segment C · Independent research

Who actually pays the bills in an online store

SQL study · PostgreSQL · 620 orders, $112,344 in sales, Jan 2024 to Jun 2025

A six-table retail schema, analyzed with cohort and window-function queries. The segment query below splits buyers by how often they come back.

CASE
    WHEN order_count = 1       THEN 'One-time buyer'
    WHEN order_count BETWEEN 2 AND 3 THEN 'Repeat buyer'
    ELSE                            'Loyal customer (4+)'
END                                           AS segment
...
ROUND(SUM(total_spend)*100.0/SUM(SUM(total_spend)) OVER(),1) AS revenue_pct

Finding: loyal customers, with four or more orders, are a third of buyers (63 of 189) and bring in 54.4% of revenue. One-time buyers are 19% of buyers and 7.5% of revenue.

Queries and data

Letting the scoring overrule a profitable backtest

Forecasting model vs. prediction markets · Python

The model turns day-ahead temperature forecasts for 7 U.S. cities into probabilities and compares them with Kalshi market prices. The first paper backtest made 7.7%. The Brier score, which measures calibration, said the market was far better, so I reported the gain as luck.

Across 4,433 settled markets, on the 1,216 where the market was genuinely unsure, the market scores 0.2114 and my model scores 0.3047. Paper return is -3.37% on 212 picks. My rule for real money has not been met, so none has been traded.

Risk factors

Risk factors

Things you should know before you invest an interview slot.

  1. My forecasting model loses to the market.

    On contested weather markets its Brier score is 0.3047 against the market's 0.2114, and paper return is -3.37%. I won't put real money behind it until it beats the market on more than 200 contested markets with a positive paper return.

  2. My most relevant work can't be shown in full.

    The Coca-Cola tool, its code and its data belong to the company. What's on this page is what I can say publicly. I'm glad to walk through how it works in an interview.

  3. I'm early in my career.

    My analyst experience comes from internships, one operations analyst role and student government. I graduate in June 2027.

  4. Two figures in one of my own projects didn't survive a rerun.

    While this report was being prepared, the ecommerce-sql queries were rerun on the repository's own seed data. The README said loyal customers were 22% of buyers and 34% of revenue, and that November and December carried 29.5% of the year's sales. The rerun says 33.3%, 54.4% and 23.4%. This report uses the rerun.
